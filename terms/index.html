5. Orders, Payments, and Taxes

  • MealOps is a facilitator, not a seller of food. MealOps does not prepare, sell, or deliver food. When you place an order through the Services, MealOps transmits it to the restaurant or marketplace that fulfills it, collects payment from you, and pays the seller. Which of the two models below applies depends on how the restaurant is connected to MealOps and is shown to you at checkout.
  • Restaurant orders. When a restaurant is connected to MealOps directly, the restaurant, not MealOps, is the seller and retailer of record for the food and beverages you buy. MealOps facilitates the order and, as the restaurant’s limited payment collection agent, collects payment from you on the restaurant’s behalf. Your payment to MealOps satisfies your payment obligation to the restaurant.
  • Marketplace orders. Some restaurants and stores are offered through third-party delivery and ordering marketplaces. For a marketplace order, the marketplace, or the merchant selling through it, is the seller and retailer of record, and the marketplace’s own terms govern that purchase. MealOps acts as your purchasing agent: we place the order with the marketplace on your behalf, pay the marketplace from a MealOps account, and collect from you the amount the marketplace charged (item prices, taxes, delivery fees, tips, and other marketplace charges) together with the MealOps service fee. MealOps does not resell marketplace items. Marketplace prices, fees, item availability, and delivery estimates are set by the marketplace and may differ from what the marketplace shows its own customers.
  • Service and delivery fees. The MealOps service fee is our charge for facilitating your order and is included in the total shown before you check out. On marketplace orders, the MealOps service fee is combined with the marketplace’s own service fee and shown as a single “Service Fee” line at checkout and on your receipt. Delivery orders may also include delivery fees set by the restaurant, a courier partner, or the marketplace.
  • How you are charged. Every order is paid for at checkout with a payment card, processed by our payment processor, Stripe. MealOps does not store full card numbers. For restaurant orders, we place an authorization hold on your card for the order total and complete the charge when the restaurant accepts the order (or sooner for orders scheduled several days ahead, because card holds expire). Marketplace orders are charged when the order is placed with the marketplace. We will not charge more than the total you approved at checkout, and if an order does not go through, the hold is released.
  • Large orders. To stay within our per-order limit (currently $250 per order, or a lower limit a restaurant sets), a large order may be split into several orders, each charged separately. Checkout shows you how an order will be split before you pay.
  • Test orders. An order marked as a test order at checkout is not sent for fulfillment and you are not charged for it.
  • Taxes. For restaurant orders, totals include applicable sales taxes based on rates provided by the restaurant or its point-of-sale system. MealOps collects these taxes for the restaurant, which is responsible for reporting and paying them, except where the law requires MealOps to collect and remit taxes as a marketplace facilitator, in which case we will. For marketplace orders, the marketplace calculates and collects taxes as the seller of record. Where the law requires it, MealOps will collect and remit sales tax on its own service fee.
